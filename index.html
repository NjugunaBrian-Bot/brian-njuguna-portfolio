01

Eliminating the #1 Driver of Repeat Contacts: Account Merge Resolution

3,000 weekly tickets eliminated Read full case study +

Executive Summary

Account merge failures were the biggest single reason customers kept contacting Branch International, and they hurt CSAT and FCR week after week. I led an investigation with other teams and a fix that dealt with the root cause, taking about 3,000 tickets a week out of the queue. A follow-up initiative aimed to cut account merge contacts by another 70%.

Context & Challenge

Customers whose national ID was already linked to another account, often one with a current, overdue, or defaulted loan, were getting stuck. They kept contacting us through the app, social media, and the call centre. The team was handling these cases one at a time, which limited how far CSAT and FCR could go.

Strategic Approach

  • Audited support logs to quantify account merge as the top contact driver
  • Investigated the underlying cases: existing loans current, overdue, or in default
  • Worked directly with Product and Engineering on a real fix instead of patching symptoms
  • Built a daily handling cadence so new cases couldn't pile up into a backlog again

02

Authoring the 2026 Customer Experience Strategy

Retention, cost, and trust in one plan Read full case study +

Executive Summary

CX work at Branch was reactive and split across teams, with no shared plan linking retention, cost, and product trust. I wrote a company strategy that gave each initiative an owner, a target, and a clear list of risks, so ideas turned into a working roadmap.

Context & Challenge

Leadership needed one plan covering three goals: keep customers who were likely to leave, lower the cost of each contact, and rebuild trust in the product after repeated service failures, without one goal working against the others.

Strategic Approach

  • Retention: a rapid fraud escalation framework aiming to escalate fraud cases within 2 minutes and resolve them within 24 hours, plus a recovery flow for unhappy customers
  • Cost optimization: a Workforce Management system to cut shrinkage from 26% toward 15%, and chatbot expansion to absorb up to 50% of chat volume
  • Trust & product usage: a "Know Your Score" loan health dashboard inside the app, and a proposal to use NLP sentiment analysis (BERT, DistilBERT, BERTopic) to pick up product feedback customers share without being asked
  • Named the support needed and the real blockers for every initiative, rather than presenting a wish list

03

Running a Team of 13 Across 7 Channels Without Dropping SLA

SLA, QA, and productivity held as the work grew Read full case study +

Executive Summary

A remote team covering the app, email, phone, SMS, social media, and the call centre in two markets needed steady SLA, QA, and coverage without burning people out. I rebuilt the team's routine around scheduling, coaching, and recognition, and first response time dropped by two thirds.

Context & Challenge

Seven channels, two markets, and 13 people meant coverage gaps were easy to miss until they showed up as SLA breaches. Leave planning, swapping days off, and overtime tracking were inconsistent, and nobody had a clear view of who was strong at what.

Strategic Approach

  • Rebuilt scheduling so leave was planned a quarter ahead and requests to swap days off were checked against coverage before approval
  • Standardized shift handovers and ticket routing to prevent queue buildup at shift changes
  • Ran biweekly 1:1s tied directly to QA audits, so coaching was specific, not generic
  • Worked with the QA team on a weekly breakdown of issue types per associate, and used it for targeted training, including strong associates coaching their peers
  • Cross-trained the team on the app, social media, reviews, and the call centre so coverage didn't depend on one or two people
  • Introduced a weekly team scorecard and monthly rewards & recognition process, posted publicly to the team
  • Built a structured onboarding plan lasting two weeks, with message targets, so new hires got up to speed on a predictable timeline

04

Finding the Causes of Low CSAT and FCR with AI Sentiment Analysis

A proposal backed by data Read full case study +

Executive Summary

CSAT and FCR were low, and manual QA could only check a small sample of conversations. Working with the QA team, I wrote the problem statement and technical proposal for an NLP sentiment analysis tool that could review every conversation and show which issues were driving dissatisfaction.

Context & Challenge

QA could only read a small slice of chats by hand, so tone problems and recurring pain points were caught late or missed. Branch already had the data (CSAT responses from Typeform, agent replies, and closed ticket details) but no way to turn it into a clear list of what to fix first.

Strategic Approach

  • Sentiment analysis (NLP): existing models (BERT, DistilBERT) trained further on Branch's own tone and language to label messages as positive, neutral, or negative
  • Emotion classification: going beyond polarity to detect anger, confusion, disappointment, and gratitude
  • Topic modeling (BERTopic): grouping conversations to show what customers complained about, or praised, most often
  • Correlation analysis: pairing sentiment with CSAT and FCR to find patterns, e.g. negative tone + long resolution time reliably predicting a low CSAT score

Impact

The proposal would move QA from random sampling to a queue ranked by priority. Negative conversations would be flagged for review automatically, root causes like "loan limit" or "KYC rejection" would surface faster, and leadership would get a live view of product feedback. It was included in the 2026 CX Strategy and taken to Engineering, where build support is still pending.

Making the Case for Self-Service PIN Resets, and a Better App Store Rating

Google Play rating: 4.2 → 4.5 stars Read full case study +

Executive Summary

PIN resets were a big, fully manual request. Every one needed an agent, which meant waiting for the customer and repetitive work for the team. I helped do the analysis we presented to Product and Engineering that made the case for a self-service PIN reset. After it launched, the app's Google Play rating went from 4.2 to 4.5.

Context & Challenge

Customers who were locked out had no way back in without contacting support, so PIN resets kept coming. Even a short wait for an agent showed up in reviews and pulled the Google Play rating down.

Strategic Approach

  • Helped analyse how many PIN reset contacts we got and how repetitive and low risk they were
  • Helped present the findings to Product and Engineering and pushed for a self-service flow
  • Represented the support team's view of failure points and edge cases during design and testing
  • Coordinated with the support team so agents could handle the smaller volume of edge cases the flow didn't cover

Cutting the Weekly CS Report From Hours to One Hour

3 to 4 hours down to about 1 Read full case study +

Executive Summary

The weekly customer service report pulled numbers from three systems and took 3 to 4 hours to build by hand. I used Gemini to speed up the data pulls and calculations, and got the same report down to about an hour without losing accuracy.

Context & Challenge

The report combined data from Metabase, Zendesk, and the call centre CRM (Mteja). I built it in Excel and later mostly in Google Sheets, and every week it ate half a morning that could have gone to coaching and follow-ups.

Strategic Approach

  • Used Gemini for the repetitive data pulls and calculations
  • My first prompts gave messy numbers, so I switched to naming the exact columns and formulas instead of asking it to work things out
  • Checked the totals against Zendesk, Metabase, and the CRM before sharing anything
